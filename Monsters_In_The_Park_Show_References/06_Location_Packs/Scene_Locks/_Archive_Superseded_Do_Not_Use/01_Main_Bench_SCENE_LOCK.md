# Main Bench – Scene Lock

## Canon Name
Main Bench

## Canon Number
1

## Purpose
Primary recurring park nook used for conversations, waiting, reflection, character beats, plans, and soft emotional moments.

## Core Layout
- The scene is a quiet circular bench nook.
- Main cobblestone path enters from foreground center and opens into a rounded circular seating area.
- Bench sits centered on the rear half of the circular nook.
- Bench faces toward camera / foreground path.
- Curved black fence wraps the rear arc behind the bench.
- Left and right garden beds frame the nook with shrubs, flowers, and trees.
- Two lamp posts flank the scene, one left and one right.
- Open foreground cobblestone area remains clear for character staging.
- Skyline sits softly in the far background behind the trees and fence.
- The layout should feel symmetrical, calm, and readable.

## Fixed Prop Positions

### Center
- Main bench is the central anchor prop.
- Bench is centered horizontally in the rear half of the circular cobblestone area.
- Bench faces camera.
- Bench should never slide left or right between views.
- Bench should never rotate away from the camera-facing orientation in the front elevation view.

### Left Side
- Left lamp post sits in the left planting edge / garden border area.
- Left lamp post is visible in wide and medium views.
- Left tree and shrub mass sit behind and around the left lamp post.
- Left curved fence follows the garden bed and rear circular arc.

### Right Side
- Right lamp post sits in the right planting edge / garden border area.
- Right tree and shrub mass sit behind and around the right lamp post.
- Right curved fence follows the garden bed and rear circular arc.

### Rear Arc
- Curved fence line is a hard continuity element.
- Rear shrubs sit behind the fence.
- Skyline remains soft and secondary.
- Do not flatten the rear arc into a straight line.

## Relative Measurements
Use these as production consistency estimates unless later replaced by measured artboard data.

- Bench width: approx. 6 ft
- Bench setback from rear curb / fence arc: approx. 2 ft
- Left lamp post to bench: approx. 8 ft
- Right lamp post to bench: approx. 8 ft
- Distance between lamp posts: approx. 26 ft
- Foreground path opening into circle: approx. 11 ft
- Bench seat height: approx. 1 ft 5 in
- Bench overall height: approx. 2 ft 9 in
- Cobblestone nook diameter: approx. 22–26 ft

## Measurement Method
- Bench width is measured from outside left edge to outside right edge of the bench seat.
- Bench setback is measured from the rear edge of the bench to the nearest inside edge of the rear cobblestone curb / fence arc.
- Lamp post distance to bench is measured from the lamp post base center to the nearest outside bench edge.
- Distance between lamp posts is measured base center to base center.
- Foreground path opening is measured from inside curb edge to inside curb edge at the entrance to the circular nook.
- Use ft and in notation.
- Use “approx.” when the value is visually inferred rather than physically measured.

## Camera Views

### Wide Plate
- Shows full circular nook, centered bench, both lamp posts, rear curved fence, shrubs, trees, and skyline.
- Use as the master layout reference.
- Keep the bench and lamp posts in their locked positions.

### Medium Plate
- Pushes in without moving props.
- Bench remains the central focal point.
- Lamp posts may stay visible if framing allows.
- Curved fence and rear shrub arc must remain readable.

### Close Plate
- Pushes toward bench and immediate cobblestone seating area.
- Lamp posts may be cropped naturally.
- Do not move lamp posts or bench to keep them visible.
- Do not redesign the bench angle.

## Character Performance Zones

### A – Bench Conversation Zone
Near and directly in front of the center bench. Use for seated characters, quiet conversations, emotional beats, waiting, and reveals.

### B – Main Meeting / Space Action Zone
Open foreground cobblestone area. Use for group staging, arrivals, short action beats, and characters entering the bench nook.

### C – Left Side Interaction Zone
Left planting and lamp post edge. Use for side entrances, hiding, small visual gags, or reaction beats.

### D – Right Side Interaction Zone
Right planting and lamp post edge. Use for side entrances, reaction beats, or secondary characters waiting off to the side.

### E – Upstage Bench / Back Arc Zone
Along the rear bench line and curved fence edge. Use for quiet background action, pauses, or characters entering from behind the bench line.

## Do Not Move
- Center bench
- Left lamp post
- Right lamp post
- Curved rear fence line
- Cobblestone circular curb
- Foreground path opening
- Rear shrub border
- Tree / shrub masses
- Skyline placement

## Style Lock
- Muted watercolor
- Dusty sage greens
- Warm beige parchment
- Soft ink lines
- Low contrast
- Light transparent foliage
- Gentle storybook texture
- No high saturation
- No hard poster-like outlines
- No glossy rendering

## Generation Rules
- Do not redesign the layout.
- Do not move the bench between shots.
- Do not move lamp posts between shots.
- Do not turn the circular nook into a straight path.
- Do not remove the rear curved fence.
- Do not crowd the center bench with props.
- Keep foreground open for characters.
- Push in by cropping from the approved wide plate, not by rebuilding the scene.
- Characters occupy the open cobblestone area and bench zone, not the fixed prop positions.
