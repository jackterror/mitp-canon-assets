# Big Tree / Shade Spot – Scene Lock

Big Tree / Shade Spot

## Canon Number
4

## Keeper Status

Use this sheet as the visual authority for the Big Tree / Shade Spot layout, prop placement, performance zones, and technical-sheet styling.

## Purpose
Recurring quiet shaded location used for calm conversations, hiding, thinking, napping, private moments, emotional pauses, and small character interactions away from the chaos of the main park.

## Core Layout
- The scene is a shaded cobblestone nook anchored by a massive old tree on the left.
- The tree canopy spreads across the upper-left and center of the frame.
- Large exposed roots sit around the tree base.
- A bench sits under the tree, slightly right of the trunk and roots.
- A lamp post sits on the far-left side near the fence and planting.
- A curved black fence wraps the rear and right side of the location.
- The right side opens into a path exit zone.
- The path exit should read as a small continuation out of the nook, not a major road.
- Cobblestone paving fills the main performance area.
- Skyline stays soft and distant in the background.
- The location should feel quiet, shaded, protected, and slightly hidden.

## Fixed Prop Positions

### Tree
- Massive tree is the dominant anchor.
- Tree sits left of center.
- Canopy spreads across the top-left and upper-middle of the scene.
- Tree roots extend into the cobblestone area.
- Do not move, shrink, or simplify the tree.
- Do not remove the visible roots.

### Bench
- Bench sits under the tree, slightly right of the trunk and roots.
- Bench faces camera / foreground in the front elevation.
- Bench is the main alternate interaction point.
- Bench should remain partially sheltered by the canopy.
- Do not move bench into the open center.
- Do not rotate bench away from the scene logic.

### Lamp Post
- Lamp post sits on the far-left side near the left fence and shrub line.
- Lamp post is secondary to the tree.
- It should stay left of the bench and left of the main trunk mass.
- Do not move the lamp post closer to the center.

### Fence Line
- Fence line runs behind the tree and bench.
- Fence continues around the right side of the nook.
- Right-side fence has a harder angled break near the path exit, not a long smooth curve.
- Fence should frame the nook and reinforce the right-side path opening.
- Do not flatten the rear fence into a straight line.
- Do not turn the right fence into a long sweeping curve.

### Cobblestone Curb / Path Geometry
- Cobblestone area opens wide in the foreground and middle ground.
- The right/back path exit is a small path opening.
- The top-down path opening should show only a short visible continuation before fading out.
- Do not create a long visible road.
- Do not make the path exit half-width or gate-like.
- The exit should match the marked keeper logic: a modest opening that continues out of the scene.

## Relative Measurements

- Depth to back / upstage fence: approx. 18 ft
- Curb height: approx. 4 in
- Bench width: approx. 6 ft
- Bench seat height: approx. 1 ft 5 in
- Lamp post height: approx. 9–10 ft
- Path exit opening: approx. 6–8 ft wide

## Measurement Method
- Tree canopy diameter is measured across the visible canopy spread in the front elevation.
- Foreground path / cobblestone width is measured curb-to-curb across the primary open performance area.
- Bench to lamp post distance is measured from nearest bench edge to lamp post base center.
- Bench to fence distance is measured from bench back edge to nearest fence line.
- Depth to back is measured from foreground performance edge to rear/upstage fence line.
- Curb height is measured from cobblestone surface to top of curb.
- Bench width is measured from outside left edge to outside right edge.
- Lamp post height is estimated from ground/base to top cap.
- Path exit opening is measured between the two fence/curb breaks at the right/back exit.
- Use ft and in notation.
- Use “approx.” when visually inferred.

### Character Performance Zones
Use only four zones for this location.

#### A – Attractor Character Interaction Point
Bench as alternate interaction point.

Placement:
- On or directly around the bench.
- Do not place A in the left empty shrub/ground area.
- Do not add a second A.

#### B – Main Meeting / Space Action Zone
Open mid-ground area for primary staging.

Placement:
- Center cobblestone area, slightly pushed back from the foreground.
- Should not sit too far forward.
- Should not drift under the tree canopy.
- Keep it in the main open performance area.

#### C – Right Side Interaction Zone
Near the right-side fence and shrubbery.

Placement:
- Keep C along the right fence/shrub zone.
- Do not move C into the center path.
- Do not remove the right-side interaction space.

#### D – Path / Exit Path Zone
Path exit leading out of the area toward the park beyond.

Placement:
- D should sit at the right/back path opening.
- D can be a simple letter marker without a large dashed region if the path sliver is narrow.
- If highlighted, the dashed shape must follow the path exit corridor.
- Do not create a big purple blob or canopy region.
- Do not turn D into an under-tree chilling zone.
